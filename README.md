Credit Card Customer & Transaction Analysis | Power BI Portfolio Project
Project Overview
This project is a credit card customer and transaction analysis created as a Business Intelligence portfolio project.
The dashboard was used to understand:
- revenue performance over time
- customer segments
- card-category performance
- transaction behaviour
- spending patterns
- geographic performance
The objective was not just to build charts, but to turn the dashboard into a business story with practical recommendations.
Dashboard KPIs
KPI	Value
Revenue	$55.3M

Interest Earned	$7.84M
Transaction Count	~655.7K


Key Business Insights
1. Revenue remained relatively stable
Quarterly revenue stayed within a fairly narrow range:
- Q1: $14.0M
- Q2: $13.8M
- Q3: $14.2M
- Q4: $13.3M
Q3 was the strongest quarter, while Q4 was the weakest. Transaction count followed a similar pattern, dropping from 166.6K in Q3 to 161.6K in Q4.
Business takeaway: I would investigate whether the Q4 decline is seasonal or caused by lower customer activity.
2. Blue cards are the main revenue driver
Blue cards generated approximately $46M of revenue, far ahead of Silver, Gold and Platinum.
Business takeaway: The Blue-card customer base is clearly important for revenue, but this also creates product concentration risk. Customer retention and profitability should be closely monitored.
3. Swipe transactions dominate
Revenue by transaction method was approximately:
- Swipe: $35M
- Chip: $17M
- Online: $3M
Business takeaway: Online transactions are relatively small compared with physical transactions. Increasing digital adoption could be an opportunity to improve engagement and convenience.
4. Businessmen are the largest customer-job segment
Businessmen generated approximately $17.4M, followed by:
- White-collar: $10.1M
- Self-employed: $8.3M
- Government: $8.1M
- Blue-collar: $6.9M
- Retirees: $4.5M
Business takeaway: Businessmen, White-collar and Self-employed customers are good segments to investigate for targeted retention and cross-selling.
5. Everyday spending is important
The largest expenditure categories were:
- Bills: $13.8M
- Entertainment: $9.5M
- Fuel: $9.3M
- Grocery: $8.6M
- Food: $8.3M
- Travel: $5.9M
Business takeaway: Customers are using their cards heavily for everyday and recurring expenses. This could support targeted rewards and merchant partnerships around those categories.
6. The 40–50 age group is the largest displayed segment
Revenue for the displayed age groups was approximately:
- 40–50: $24M
- 50–60: $18M
- 30–40: $10M
Business takeaway: The 40–50 customer group represents an important revenue segment and could be studied further for retention and premium-product opportunities.
7. Texas and California lead the displayed states
Approximate revenue:
- Texas: $12.7M
- California: $12.5M
- New York: $11.1M
- Florida: $9.8M
Business takeaway: Texas and California can be used as benchmark markets when comparing customer acquisition and engagement in other states.
Recommendations
Based on the dashboard, I would recommend:
1. Investigate the Q4 decline by customer segment, card category and transaction method.
2. Increase digital adoption by targeting high-value customers who mainly use swipe transactions.
3. Protect the Blue-card customer base while identifying customers who may be suitable for higher-tier products.
4. Create segment-specific campaigns for Businessmen, White-collar and Self-employed customers.
5. Add profitability and risk metrics before using the dashboard for major management decisions.

Data Limitations
This portfolio project is based on the supplied dashboard screenshots rather than the underlying transaction-level dataset.
Therefore:
- Dashboard values are treated as approximate where they are rounded.
- Exact calculations should be validated against the source data.
- Some metric definitions are not visible in the dashboard.
- Profitability cannot be calculated without additional information such as losses, costs, delinquency and acquisition cost.
